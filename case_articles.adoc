
= Nominativ vs Akkusativ

== der Mann sieht den Hund.
the man sees the dog.


here the man is the one who sees, the man is the 'doer' and the Dog is being  
seen

== den Hund sieht der Mann.
the man sees the dog.

here also the man is the one who sees, the man is the 'doer' and the
Dog is being seen.


== WTF?
the roles are not necessarily determined by the position of the words 
like in English the Nominative is the one who does the verb and 
the Akkusative (the one accused of the verb), is what the verb is being acted on. 


the difference of :

"den Hund sieht der man."
and 
"der Mann sieht den Hund."
is what the phrasing emphasis is.
like saying:
"THE MAN!! sees the Dog."
or 
"the man sees THE DOG!!."

= WHAT THE FUCK IS A DATIV !!!!!!!!!

now insted of seeing the dog,
we want to get rid of the stupid dog

== Ich gibe den Hund
i give 'the dog'

but this rases a question. who did i give the dog to?

if im the dog giver, and the dog is the dog, who is the dog reciver here?

==== so we say
== Ich give den Hund dem Mann
i gave the dog to the man 

and just like befor we can do all the permutations of the sentence

**Ich gibe den Hund dem Mann**

**Ich gibe den Hund dem Mann**

**den Hund gibe Ich dem Mann**

**den Hund gibe dem Mann Ich**

**dem Mann gibe Ich den Hund**

**dem Mann gibe den Hund Ich**

=== they all mean the same thing
the dog was transfered from me to a specific man


we can also have more dative nouns here:

== Ich gibe den Hund dem Mann in dem Park

*in dem park* is also the place that the dog giving took place and is dative here

the dative can also be created by certain prepositions:

mit  → Dativ

bei  → Dativ

von  → Dativ

aus  → Dativ

zu   → Dativ


so:

== Ich bin mit dem Mann.
I am with the man.

== Ich komme aus dem Haus.
I come from the house.

=== some prepositions can use either Akkusativ or Dativ:

* auf
* in
* an
* über
* unter
* vor
* hinter
* neben
* zwischen
